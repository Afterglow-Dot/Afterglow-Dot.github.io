> "In modern society, there is a prevailing sentiment that in the eager pursuit of personal success, we tend to lose friends along the way."

---
## I. The Conflict Between Success and Friendship

Many people view friendship as a comfort. **However, more often than not, in the eager pursuit of personal success, we lose friends, and this has become part of the process.**

In our daily lives, the accompanying chaos and endless turmoil have become a major obstacle to pursuing stable friendships. As students, we often use this as an excuse to be complacent about losing those we once regarded as beloved friends.

## II. Re-evaluating Life

**We should not view life as a race where everyone runs alone towards some form of success; instead, we must relearn to treasure the friendships built along the way, and we must re-evaluate the harm brought by extreme individual freedom.**

## III. The Normalization of Losing Friends

As a society, we advocate the concept of "losing friends on the path to success." Just like the argument we often heard in high school: **"Don't worry about losing friendships, just focus on your studies and get into a good university. In the long run, that's what matters most."** This concept is not just a unique personal experience. It has been preached by many success stories and is deeply embedded in our understanding of growth.

This continuous desire for success has blinded us, preventing us from seeing through the chaotic changes accompanying our daily lives. We expect tangible success, rewards, higher positions, and better grades. To achieve success, we lose friends, and sadly, this has become normalized.

## IV. Friendship as a Spiritual Anchor

**We cannot be complacent about losing friendships, because friendship is our spiritual anchor.**

Of course, we should pursue success, challenge ourselves, and break through limits. But contrary to that: **Acting alone will not make you more successful than those who achieve success with the help of others, nor will it make you superior.**

**Strive to foster healthy interpersonal relationships, and maintain them even in a busy schedule where there seems to be no time for others. Although we live in a society that values independent pursuit of achievement, having someone support and love you along the way is far more important than any final result.**

We need people who tangibly support us. We must foster these connections and treasure the companionship that accompanies our journey. When we face worldly pressures and need a comfort blanket, we should have someone to confide in. True success is not just about reaching the top alone; it's about who is standing by your side when you get there.

## V. Conclusion

As I mentioned in my first blog post, we must "see the world, see others, and see ourselves." Seeing others means recognizing the value of our connections with them. The best state of life is having the ability to stay clear-headed amidst the chaos and to share our journey with others.
