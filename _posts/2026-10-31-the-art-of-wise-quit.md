> "We have all repeatedly heard the phrase 'quitters never win', but sometimes, the best decision is to cut your losses in time."

---
## I. The Sunk-Cost Trap

We are all susceptible to the sunk-cost fallacy. This unconscious bias leads us to worry excessively about what we will lose if we move forward, failing to fully consider the cost of not moving forward. To counteract this bias, we must consciously refocus our minds on the gains, so that we can weigh our options more objectively.

## II. Illusions and Traps

The illusion of control can also interfere with our best judgment. Especially when combined with a "never give up" mindset, that sense of having more control over something than is actually the case makes us double down when we should pull out. We must recognize that **"expending energy and emotion on things you can't control is both exhausting and makes you feel powerless."** While you may be able to influence others and various environments, you cannot force them to change or act according to your will.

**Knowing what you can control and what you can't is crucial for making high-quality decisions about whether to give up or persevere.**

Furthermore, if you strongly identify with the identity of "never being a quitter", you may be trapped in a course of action due to a narrow and rigid self-concept. This is often a self-imposed blinding.

## III. Seeking External Perspectives

How can we make better decisions? **"Ideally, you should solicit the opinions of those who don't have as much invested in your decision as you do."** You can also seek out hard data that clearly shows the true probability of success in any endeavor you are considering.

When things don't develop as we wish, we easily fall into excessive self-criticism. After all, **"no one likes the feeling of having made a bad decision, especially when the quality of wise decision-making is highly esteemed by you."** At this point, the most effective strategy is to give yourself a little breathing room. **Practicing self-compassion can not only enhance your resilience, improve emotional intelligence and empathy for others, but also improve your performance and help you develop a growth mindset.**

**Rather than repeatedly agonizing and criticizing yourself, it's better to focus on what you can learn from this experience.**

## IV. Final Thoughts

It is undeniable that **perseverance can help us move towards success.** But for your physical and mental well-being, **it is crucial to learn to distinguish between "giving up too early" and "holding on to a losing battle".** **Although letting go can sometimes be hard, it will free up your time, energy, and mental space, allowing you to conceive new possibilities and pursue new opportunities.**

As I mentioned in my first blog post, "See the world, see others, see yourself." To "see yourself" is to see through your own biases and rigid self-cognition, and to learn the wisdom of strategic retreat. **Letting go is not always a sign of weakness; sometimes, it is the highest quality decision.**
